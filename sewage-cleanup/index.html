Few emergencies in a building are as hazardous as sewage backing up into it. Raw sewage contains dangerous bacteria, viruses, parasites and other pathogens, including E. coli, hepatitis, salmonella and giardia, that pose serious health risks to anyone who touches contaminated materials. Across Draper, Draper Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. Because of it, cleanup follows the toughest rules in the trade, which means full protective gear, barriers to contain the area, tearing out any porous material that met sewage and disinfecting all that is left. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

Sewage Cleanup: What Homeowners Ask

We typically recommend temporary relocation during sewage cleanup, especially if the contamination in your home is extensive. Airborne pathogens and the chemicals used for decontamination can both pose health risks. After seeing your situation firsthand, our team tells you what makes sense.
Some things can be kept and others cannot. Glass, sealed tile, metal and other hard surfaces that do not absorb water are normally salvageable after disinfection. Porous materials that contacted sewage, including carpet, padding, drywall, insulation, mattresses and upholstered furniture, have to be discarded. Each discarded item is documented, giving you a record to use with your insurer.

The Benefits of Professional Sewage Cleanup

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

How to Tell You Need Sewage Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Multiple drains in your home backing up at the same time
  • Family members falling ill, especially with gastrointestinal trouble, for no obvious reason
  • Toilets or drains that gurgle whenever you run water somewhere else
  • Wet spots on floors or walls accompanied by foul odors
  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them
  • Drain openings or basement slabs coated with visible sewage residue
  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Never pour grease, oil or food waste down your drains, since these cause blockages
  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog
  • Clear tree roots near sewer lines, which can infiltrate and block the pipes
  • Install a backwater valve on your sewer connection to prevent backups from the municipal system
  • Find your sewer cleanout now and check its condition, so it can be reached in an emergency
  • Stormwater from a sump or downspout should never drain into the sewer, since the extra volume invites a backup
