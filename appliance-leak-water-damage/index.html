Appliance Leak Water Damage Help in Draper

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. When the supply hose on a washing machine splits, water flows under full pressure until somebody turns it off. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. Behind a refrigerator, the thin tube feeding the ice maker may drip for months. No two of these call for the same cleanup.

On every Draper job, the first step is moving the appliance and tracing the path the water took. Kitchen leaks tend to end up beneath the cabinets and underneath the floor. With a laundry leak we look at the rooms next to it and the wall behind the washer, plus the ceiling below when the laundry sits on an upper floor. We extract what is standing, measure what has soaked in, and set drying equipment sized to the wet area.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut the Machine DownIf the outlet and the floor next to it are both dry, end the cycle and take the plug out of the wall. When either one is wet, turn the power off at the breaker.
  2. Shut Off the Water LineBehind a washer, on the wall, are separate hot and cold valves. The dishwasher valve is under the kitchen sink. Refrigerator lines have a small valve behind or below.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterGetting reachable water up with a mop or towels stops it from moving under the cabinets and the floor. Use a vacuum only if it is a wet and dry model.
  4. Keep the Door ClosedDo not open a washer or dishwasher that is still full. When the door opens mid-cycle, the full load of water can pour out on the floor.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartKeep the part that failed, whether a hose, a fitting or a valve, and get photos of it. Your insurer and the appliance maker may both ask to see it.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • A dishwasher that was installed out of level or with a supply connection left loose.
  • A clogged defrost drain or cracked drain pan that lets a refrigerator leak onto the floor.
  • Plastic or copper ice maker lines that kink, crack or loosen at the fitting.
  • Rubber washing machine supply hoses that weaken with age and split under pressure.
  • The drain hose of the washer pulling free of the standpipe, or the standpipe backing up from a clog.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Adjoining Rooms

A washer hose can release water for hours. That water moves beneath walls into bedrooms, closets and hallways, soaking pad and carpet far from the laundry room.

Kitchen Cabinets

From below, a leaking dishwasher wets the toe kicks and the side panels of the cabinets. The panels swell and separate, and wet cabinet boxes can hold moisture against the wall for weeks.

Downstairs Ceiling Damage

In a home with an upstairs laundry, leak water passes through the floor and pools on the ceiling underneath. Sagging, staining and soaked insulation above the drywall are the usual result.

Floors and What Is Under Them

Water slides beneath laminate, vinyl plank and hardwood. Cupping and swelling show up in the boards, and the subfloor can stay damp long after the surface appears dry.

Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Appliance Leak Water Damage

When a machine overflows unexpectedly or a hose bursts, that is typically treated as sudden and accidental, which many homeowners policies cover. Payment typically goes toward the damage the water did, and the appliance or the part that failed is not included. A claim is harder when an ice maker line or dishwasher seeped slowly, since coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. How old the hose was and how it was maintained may also come up with some insurers. Check your policy, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.

Pricing

What Drives the Price

  • If the flooring and cabinets have to come out or can be dried where they are.
  • Type of water, since gray discharge requires sanitizing and clean supply water does not.
  • The number of rooms affected, counting a ceiling under a laundry on an upper floor.
  • Which appliance leaked and whether it was a fast flood or a slow seep.
